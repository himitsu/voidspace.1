00602.08.26Port Dickson Mayangsari Campsite

Ten thousand watts against a sea breeze

The night

Mayangsari Campsite, Port Dickson. Twenty-nine degrees, humid, with a steady evening breeze coming off the water. We wanted three things: whether the wind moves the flame, what the burners actually put out, and whether the fuel stays honest in that much moisture.

Unit
003 Half Unit IGT Stove
Conditions
29°C, humid, sea breeze
Focus
Wind, output, fuel stability
Setting up

Form factor

36 × 12.5 × 8 cm, 1.33 kg. It disappears into a standard gear bag — no dedicated slot, no rearranging around it.

IGT integration

Drops flush into a half-unit IGT slot with no play. On sand or grass, the cooking surface stays where you put it.

Build

Alloy and stainless steel, rigid past what the weight suggests. Rated to roughly 20 kg, and it took heavy cookware without complaint.

Heat

Ignition

Piezoelectric ceramic ignition, lit on the first click in tropical humidity. The lighter stayed in the bag all night.

Output

The five-ring array pushes up to 10,000 W. It sears a large pan evenly, with no hot spot in the middle, and the breeze never took the flame with it.

Preheat loop

The integrated loop keeps the flame clean and steady — no sputtering, no drift in delivery as the canister cools.

Fuel

Canister

Standard EN417 threaded canisters. Nothing proprietary, nothing to source ahead.

Keep the canister upright

Always run the canister standing straight. Upright keeps liquid fuel out of the line, which is what prevents flare-ups and holds the vapour supply consistent.

Burn time

Around 40 minutes of high-output cooking from a 230 g canister. Plan the gas accordingly if you are feeding a group.

Verdict

A compact powerhouse for coastal cooking: high heat, stable footing, packs down to nothing. Watch the canister placement and it runs all night without asking for attention.

About
Ten years of sleeping outside taught us what to leave behind. VOIDSPACE.1 is what was left.
01 — Who

We are campers first. For more than ten years we have been going up into the highlands, off the roads, into weather that most people drive home from — and for the last five, we have been putting equipment into other people's hands and watching what happened to it.

You learn quickly which things come back broken, which things get returned unused, and which things people refuse to give up. That list is short. Everything we make now comes from it.

VOIDSPACE.1 is the answer to a question we kept asking ourselves in the dark: what would we carry if nobody was watching, and price was not the conversation?
